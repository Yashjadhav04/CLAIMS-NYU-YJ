# Medicare Part D Cost Trend: Briefing Memo

Source: CMS public data (data.cms.gov). Figures are gross cost before rebates for all of Medicare Part D and do not represent any single plan. Generated by `python -m partd.briefing`.

## Summary
Gross Part D cost per member per month rose from $341 (2020) to $442 (2024), then was flat from 2023 to 2024. Total spending reached $289B in 2024. The flat 2024 result reflects two offsetting movements: higher claims per member and lower cost per claim, the latter led by insulin list-price reductions.

## Key findings
1. **Cost per claim, not utilization, drove 2020 to 2023.** Claims per member barely moved (2,556 to 2,596 per 1,000 member months); spend per claim went from $133 to $171.
2. **2024 reversed the pattern.** Spend per claim fell to $169. Insulin accounts for about $8B of a $29B negative price effect; the remainder is spread across roughly 2,700 drugs and has not been attributed.
3. **GLP-1 drugs are the largest growth category.** $7.2B to $27.9B between 2020 and 2024 (9.7% of spend).
4. **Negotiated prices take effect in 2026 for ten drugs.** These drugs represented $61B (21%) of 2024 spend. Applying the announced cuts to 2024 volume would reduce spend by about $37B at list-price level. Preliminary Q1 2026 data show spend per claim already 30% to 71% lower for most of these drugs.
5. **State-level variation is material.** Excluding DC (distorted by cross-border prescribing), NY is highest at $590 and WY lowest at $235 per member per month, a 2.5x spread. These data cannot distinguish utilization, price, drug mix or plan mix as the cause.

## Recommended next steps and monitoring
- Rebase the trend on the 2025 annual file when CMS publishes it; the preliminary quarterly figure ($351B) is not comparable to the annual series.
- Confirm whether the early decline in spend per claim for the negotiated drugs persists as Q2 2026 and later quarters are released.
- Enrollment mix: MA-PD share rose from 47% to 57% of Part D enrollees between 2020 and 2024.

## Limitations
- Gross cost excludes rebates; net cost to a plan is lower and cannot be determined from these data.
- Dose units for injectables can change with strength and packaging, so unit-price movements for insulin and GLP-1 drugs partly reflect definitional changes.
- State cost is by prescriber location; enrollment by member residence.
- The 2026 reductions compare announced negotiated prices with list prices; the Q1 2026 check is directional only.
